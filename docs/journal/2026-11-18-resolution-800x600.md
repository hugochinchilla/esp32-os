# A second screen size: 800x600

2026-11-18. Backlog item 68. Built and tested on the PC; the firmware and
the BIOS compile and link in the cloud (ESP-IDF v5.5). **Nothing here has
run on the board.** Whether the board and the monitor hold 800x600 is the
question the owner asked, and only the board turn answers it; every figure
below is worked out on paper or measured on the PC and says so.

## What was asked

The owner, in the project chat:

> add a tab to the display config dialog, current content sits on
> "Appearance", and add "Resolution" with a switcher for 800x600, let's see
> if the board can handle that

The coordinator added what the frame-budget work (item 63) had just found:
the board shows 59.52 pictures a second, a piece of the desktop's work has
16.8 ms, and about 2% of them already take longer. So the cost of the larger
picture is to be reported honestly: memory, timing, pictures a second.

## What was found

- The video adapter (`firmware/os/main/vga.c`) had one mode written into it
  as constants: 640x480, a 25 MHz pixel clock, 800x525 in all, 16,800 us a
  picture. The `esp_lcd` RGB panel is made once with those figures. A mode
  cannot be changed on a made panel; it is deleted and made again.
- The panel is fed from our own picture memory through two bounce buffers of
  ten lines, filled by an interrupt on core 1. The size of a picture must be
  a whole number of bounce buffers: 307,200 / 6,400 = 48, and
  480,000 / 8,000 = 60, so ten lines works for both.
- 640x480 was assumed in more places than the adapter: the canvas's scratch
  band and fade table (640 wide), the power-down picture, the remote
  screen's size (measured once at start), the PC window, the picture
  checker, the test rig.
- The Display dialog was one page of fourteen controls. `os::ui` had no
  tabs. The Widget gallery draws tabs of its own inside one widget; nothing
  a dialog could use.
- The standard timing for 800x600 at 60 Hz needs a 40 MHz pixel clock, which
  is 160 MHz / 4 exactly: the board can make it without rounding. 25 MHz was
  160 / 6.4, already rounded by the driver.

## What 800x600 costs, on paper

| | 640x480 | 800x600 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pixels | 307,200 | 480,000 | 56% more to paint and to send |
| Pixel clock | 25 MHz | 40 MHz | 60% more bytes a second out of the external RAM |
| Whole picture | 800x525 | 1056x628 | |
| One picture | 16,800 us (59.52 a second) | 16,579 us (60.32 a second) | 221 us less for each piece of work |
| Picture memory (external) | 307,200 bytes | 480,000 bytes | 172,800 more, **in both modes** (see below) |
| Bounce buffers (internal) | 2 x 6,400 | 2 x 8,000 | 3,200 more, only while in 800x600 |
| A refill every | 320 us | 264 us | less room for anything that holds interrupts off |
| Canvas band and fade table (internal) | 7,680 + 2,560 | 9,600 + 3,200 | 2,560 more, in both modes |

What this predicts, to be checked on the board:

- **Painting.** The work that was 30 to 59 ms for a large window before the
  faster drawing (item 65) grows with the window. A maximised window has 59%
  more pixels (800x572 against 640x452). More pieces of work will be over
  the budget; how many is the benchmark's to say
  (`tools/bench/desktop_frames.py`).
- **Scanout.** The interrupt copied 307,200 bytes 59.52 times a second,
  about 5% of core 1 as measured. At 480,000 bytes 60.32 times a second it
  should be about 8 to 10%. This is a guess from the ratio of bytes.
- **The external RAM's bus.** The copy reads 28.9 MB a second from the PSRAM
  instead of 18.3. If the bus cannot keep up while the desktop also writes
  to it, refills are missed and the picture tears or rolls. This is the
  likeliest way for 800x600 to fail, and `tools/dbg.py misses` shows it.
- **The signal.** 40 MHz through the resistor ladder is past what was
  tried on the bench (25 MHz). Edges may be soft. Only the monitor tells.

## The choices

**How the change happens** was the owner's, on a decision card: at once,
with a box that asks whether to keep it and goes back after 15 s with no
answer. Not chosen: at the next start (if the monitor refuses the picture
there is no way back but the debug link), and at once with no question.

The rest are the agent's and are recorded as to confirm in
`docs/decisions/screen-sizes.md`:

- **40 MHz and 60 Hz**, not 36 MHz and 56 Hz. The slower mode would ask 10%
  less of the memory bus, but 160 / 36 is not a whole number and fewer
  monitors take 56 Hz. If 60 Hz fails on the board, 56 Hz is one more row
  in the table to try.
- **The picture memory is always 480,000 bytes.** Its address never changes,
  so the remote screen and the screenshot, which read it from other tasks,
  can never meet freed memory, and a change cannot fail for lack of a large
  enough piece of external RAM. The price is 172.8 KB less free external RAM
  in 640x480 too. The other way (give back, ask again) saves that memory
  while in the small mode and was not chosen because every reader would have
  to be stopped first and the change could fail half-way. The BIOS asks for
  one mode only and keeps the small memory.
- **The saved size is put on when the desktop starts.** The BIOS and the
  starting picture stay 640x480, so a monitor on a board set to 800x600
  finds the signal a second time at each start. Reading the desktop's
  settings from the BIOS was left out.
- **A remote viewer is dropped** at a change and connects again at the new
  size. The browser's viewer page does not connect again by itself.
- **Windows are refitted**, not scaled: maximised ones fill the new screen,
  a window that was centred is centred again, the others are pulled inside,
  a resizable one made smaller if it must. Icons are laid out again; the
  taskbar follows the screen.
- **Tabs as one control** that says which page is in front; the program
  places or hides its own controls. A control with an empty place is not
  shown and Tab and Alt+letter pass it over. Pages that own their controls
  would be tidier and change far more of `os::ui`.

## What was built

- **Modes in the Display port** (`core/include/core/ports.hpp`):
  `VideoMode` (size, pixel clock, time of one picture), `mode_count`,
  `mode_at`, `mode`, `set_mode`. The adapter keeps a table of two
  (`firmware/os/main/vga.c`); `vga_set_mode` deletes the panel and makes it
  again with the new timing, on core 1 where the refill interrupt lives. The
  system log gets a line, "screen: 800x600, pixel clock 40000000 Hz
  (changed in N us)".
- **The window manager** (`core/src/wm/wm.cpp`): `set_video_mode` only notes
  what is wanted; the change is made at the top of the next tick, outside
  any painting and before the piece of work that is measured. It stops the
  screen saver, closes menus, lets go of the mouse, changes the mode, takes
  the new budget from the mode's picture time, pulls the pointer inside,
  refits the windows, tells the panels (`PanelNews::Screen`) and paints
  everything.
- **OS calls** (`docs/os-calls.md`): `os::video_mode_count`,
  `video_mode_at`, `video_mode`, `set_video_mode`.
- **Tabs** (`wm::Tabs`, `os::ui::tabs`, `app::Tabs`): the strip and the
  frame of the page, in the bevelled and the flat themes; a click, Left and
  Right on the tabs, Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab from anywhere in the
  window.
- **The Display tool** (`core/src/shell/display.cpp`): pages Appearance and
  Resolution. The preview stays on both. Resolution has the list of sizes
  and, for the one picked, what it costs: pixels, picture memory, pictures a
  second, pixel clock, time for one picture. OK and Apply change the screen
  and open the question, a program window of its own: "The screen is now
  800 x 600. Do you want to keep this setting? Going back to 640 x 480 in
  15 seconds.", Keep and Go back (the default, and Escape). Keep writes
  `[screen] mode=800x600` to `C:\.config\desktop.ini`.
- **The remote screen** (`core/src/net/rfb.cpp`) measures the screen again
  after a change and drops its viewer.
- **The debug link**: request 0x19 lists the modes and can change to one;
  `tools/dbg.py mode` and `tools/dbg.py mode 800x600`. Nothing is saved and
  no question is asked, so a board turn can always get back to 640x480.
- **The power-down picture** fills the whole screen and sits in the middle.
- **On the PC** the test display offers the same two modes; the desktop
  program's window changes size with it.

## How it was tested

- PC tests: the modes and the refit of windows (`test_wm`), the tabs and
  their keys, the remote screen after a change (`test_rfb`), the debug
  request against the simulator (`tools/test_dbg_sim.py`). The block counts
  of the Display tool in `test_os` and `test_lifecycle` went from 15 to 20:
  five more controls.
- Pictures: the Display pictures were drawn again (the page frame is new),
  and eight new ones, `37y` to `38f`, show the Resolution page, the question
  at 15 s and at 1 s, the dialog after going back, the desktop and a
  maximised Editor at 800x600. `tools/check_pictures.py` now takes either
  size. The dialog was looked at in the four themes.
- The firmware (1,753,216 bytes) and the BIOS (304,608 bytes) compile and
  link in the cloud. Not run.

## What was learned

- Once the port had the notion of a mode, most of the work was finding
  where 640 and 480 had been assumed. A change that is only noted and then
  made at the top of a tick needed no lock anywhere.
- A list that is hidden by an empty place and shown again had to be laid
  out again (`place_control` now does it); it showed one row and a broken
  scroll bar.

## What is left

- **The board turn**: does the monitor lock, are refills missed, how much
  of core 1 the scanout takes, the frame benchmark in both modes, free
  memory in both, the time the change takes, the question's 15 s on the
  real thing. The request is in the board queue.
- If 60 Hz does not hold: try 56 Hz at 36 MHz, or drop the mode.
- The BIOS and the starting picture in the saved size.
- A viewer that follows the change without connecting again.
- `docs/DECISIONS.md` still says the picture is 640x480 under "Always
  applies"; it changes when the board has shown that 800x600 holds.

## On the board (2026-11-19)

The board turn (batch 18) ran the request. As the coordinator reported it to
this thread; the full figures are the board thread's to record:

- The monitor holds 800x600 with a steady picture.
- Idle, no video refill is missed.
- Under load at 800x600 (a window dragged while Chess thinks, and the
  benchmark's other busy parts) 270 refills were missed. At 640x480 the
  figure stays what it was.
- Free external RAM is 174 KB lower in both modes, as worked out.

So the board can show 800x600 and cannot yet keep it clean when busy. The
owner's answer, in the project chat: "fusionala dejando 640 por defecto"
(merge it, leaving 640 as the default). Nothing had to change for that: a
board starts in 640x480 unless a size was kept in `desktop.ini`, and the
board's file was left without one. 800x600 stays a choice in Display >
Resolution. Why refills are missed under load at 40 MHz, and whether 56 Hz
or a cheaper scanout cures it, is left for a later item.
