# The refills missed at 800x600

2026-11-19, a thread of its own, on a branch (`claude/project-thread-e2bt47`).
Backlog item 68, the part it left open.

## What was asked

The owner, in the project chat: "vamos con 60, y los refrescos a 800x600,
vnc_tool tambien". Three pieces of work, one thread each; this is the second.
The coordinator's note: 800x600 misses about 270 video refills under load
(a window dragged while Chess thinks) against none at 640x480; find the cause
and fix it if that can be done.

## What was found

No board in this thread: the cause was worked out from the figures of board
batch 18 and from reading ESP-IDF's driver (`esp_lcd_panel_rgb.c`, v5.5).
It is a reading of the evidence until the board confirms it.

What batch 18 measured at 800x600:

- Idle: no refill missed. The scanout takes 1,109 us a frame (6.7% of core
  1; 896 us and 5.3% at 640x480).
- The frame benchmark: 270 refills missed, up to 141 in 10 s, mostly in
  "drag + chess" and "drops + chess". **The worst was 73 us late, and every
  one was at lines 30 to 50.**
- On the webcam a dragged window looked steady, with no tearing.
- The desktop was busy 35.9% of the time in "drag + chess" against 26.0% at
  640x480, for nearly the same pixels painted (208,880 and 204,372).

How the scanout works. The driver sends the picture from two bounce
buffers in internal RAM, each 10 lines. When the DMA has sent one it
raises an interrupt and our `on_bounce_empty` (`vga.c`) fills that buffer
again with the piece two places further on, while the other buffer goes
out. So a refill has one period to finish: 320 us at 640x480 (6,400
bytes), 264 us at 800x600 (8,000 bytes). The picture is in external RAM,
which the processor only reaches through its 32 KB data cache; a copy of
8,000 bytes that the cache does not hold is 125 fetches of 64 bytes over
the external RAM's bus. So at the end of each refill the interrupt asks the
cache to fetch the next piece on its own (`Cache_Start_DCache_Preload`),
and the next refill is a copy from the cache: about 20 us.

Where that breaks. A miss is counted when lateness plus copy time is over
one period. With at most 73 us of lateness the copies themselves took 190 us
or more: they were not coming from the cache. And always lines 30 to 50,
the first refills the counter looks at in a frame. The order of events at
the top of a frame, from the driver's code:

1. The last buffer of a frame finishes. The interrupt fills it with the
   new frame's second piece (lines 10 to 19) and asks the cache for the
   third (lines 20 to 29).
2. The vertical blank: 28 lines at 800x600, 739 us, with no refill.
3. The first buffer of the new frame goes out: 264 us more.
4. The interrupt wants the third piece, **about 1 ms after the cache was
   asked for it**. Idle, it is still there. With the desktop painting and
   Chess thinking, 1 ms is enough for a 32 KB cache to have let it go. The
   copy then runs at the bus's speed, and the bus is shared with whatever
   else is reading and writing external RAM (the code itself runs from
   there).
5. That slow copy uses up most of its 264 us, so the fetch for the fourth
   piece (line 30) starts late and is not done when it is wanted; the same
   for line 40, less so for line 50; then the fetching has caught up.

At 640x480 the same thing happens and does not matter: 6,400 bytes at the
bus's speed fit in 320 us with time to spare. At 800x600 there is a quarter
more to copy in a sixth less time. The busier desktop (35.9% against 26.0%)
fits too: the scanout takes a larger share of the same bus, and painting
waits for it.

Were they seen? Probably not. The DMA needs the first byte of a buffer
one period after it was handed over and the last byte a period after that,
and a copy that started at most 73 us late and runs faster than the DMA
reads stays ahead of it. The counter is stricter than the screen: it asks
for the whole buffer before the DMA starts on it. That matches the steady
picture on the webcam. The margin was nearly gone all the same.

Noticed in passing, not chased: at 800x600 the standard's front porch is one
line, so the driver's end-of-frame interrupt comes 26 us after the last
buffer's. If the two were ever served in the other order the driver would
fill a buffer twice and that frame would be shown 10 lines out of place.
Nothing in the figures shows it happening (it would be logged as a miss
about 740 us late).

## The options considered

1. **Copy the third piece ahead, in the blank.** When the frame's second
   piece is handed over (step 1) the interrupt also copies the third into
   8,000 bytes of internal RAM, at whatever speed the bus allows: nothing
   waits for it for a millisecond. At step 4 the refill copies from internal
   RAM in about 20 us and the cache has nearly the whole period for the
   fourth piece. Costs 8,000 bytes of internal RAM in that mode and an
   interrupt that runs 0.1 to 0.25 ms longer once a frame, in the blank.
2. **A 64 KB data cache**, Espressif's advice for a picture in external RAM.
   Takes 32 KB of internal RAM in both modes, and a millisecond of painting
   empties 64 KB as well as 32.
3. **800x600 at 56 Hz** (36 MHz): 10% less to copy; the blank is as long.
4. **Larger bounce buffers** (20 lines): twice the period, twice the copy.
5. **Count a miss only when the DMA overtakes the copy.** The figure would
   likely drop to zero with nothing changed, and the next loss of margin
   would not be seen until it was on the screen.
6. **Leave it**, since the picture looked steady.

## What was chosen

Option 1, by the agent, recorded as to confirm
(`docs/decisions/scanout-ahead.md`): it goes at the cause (the one refill
that cannot come from the cache) and is the cheapest in memory. For
800x600 only: 640x480 missed nothing and keeps its 6,400 bytes. The counter
stays strict (not option 5). Options 2 to 4 leave the millisecond in place.

## What was built

- `firmware/os/main/vga.c`: a mode has a word `ahead`, on for 800x600. With
  it, `start_mode` asks for one bounce buffer's worth of internal RAM
  (`vga_set_mode` gives it back, after the panel and its interrupt are
  gone; without the memory the mode runs as before). In `on_bounce_empty`,
  the refill of the second piece copies the third into it instead of
  asking the cache for it, and the refill of the third piece copies from
  it. The pointer is mixed in afterwards as always. The copy ahead is not
  timed as a refill; it has a figure of its own.
- The same file and `vga.h`: the scanout figures gain the longest fill of
  each of the frame's first eight pieces and the longest copy ahead, and
  start again at a change of mode.
- `firmware/os/main/main.cpp`: one more line in the memory report
  (`tools/dbg.py memory`): `top of the frame, longest fills: ... us; copy
  ahead N us`. In 640x480, where nothing is copied ahead, that line shows
  the slow third and fourth refills under load if this reading is right.

## How it was tested

- PC tests: see the pull request (the change is in the firmware's adapter,
  which the PC does not build).
- The firmware and the BIOS compile and link in the cloud with ESP-IDF v5.5.
- **Nothing has run on the board.** The board request asks for the
  benchmark in both modes with the new line of the memory report, and for
  the misses.

## What was learned

- "Always lines 30 to 50" was the whole clue: the batch 18 report had it in
  one sentence. Where a miss is says more than how many there are.
- The cache's fetch-ahead is only as good as the time between asking and
  using. Once a frame that time is four times longer than anywhere else.

## What is left

- The board turn. If misses remain, the new line says where the long
  copies are.
- If the owner wants 800x600 as the default afterwards, that is his to say
  (`docs/decisions/screen-sizes.md`).

## On the board (2026-11-19, batch 21)

As the coordinator reported it to this thread; not merged in that turn.

- **800x600, the frame benchmark three times: no missed refill of the
  board's own** (270 in batch 18). Worst lateness 6 us. "drag + chess" busy
  36.4% (35.9% before: the copy did not make painting cheaper).
- The longest fill was still 218, 214 and 208 us in the three runs, and the
  copy ahead took 310, 296 and 295 us under load and 200 us idle. That is
  more than the 150 to 250 us guessed above, and more than one period: **a
  piece of 8,000 bytes that the cache does not hold cannot be copied inside
  264 us when the board is busy**, wherever in the frame it happens.
- 640x480: no miss, as before. The new line read `49 129 263 209 138 107
  129 131` us with no copy ahead: the third and fourth pieces are the slow
  ones, as the reading above said, and 263 us fits in 320.
- Free internal RAM 177,023 bytes at 800x600 against 188,803 at 640x480
  (the 8,000 bytes and the larger bounce buffers). Ten changes of mode leave
  the totals as they were; the largest free internal block ends at 106,496
  instead of 114,688.
- By hand at 800x600: a window dragged for 8 s with Chess thinking, and the
  Editor maximised, missed nothing. **Culebra started with the Editor
  maximised, 10 s of play: one event of 3 missed refills, 67 us late at
  worst, at line 140.** Not the top of the frame, so not what this change
  cures.

What that leaves. The once-a-frame cause is gone. What remains is the thin
margin itself: anything that makes the cache lose a piece in the middle of a
frame (a guess for the Culebra event: the program being loaded from its
file into external RAM) costs 800x600 two or three refills, and costs
640x480 nothing. Giving every refill that margin would mean keeping a piece
ahead in internal RAM all the time, a second copy of 8,000 bytes at each
refill, about twice the scanout's share of core 1 (6.7% now). Not built;
whether it is worth it is the owner's to say.
