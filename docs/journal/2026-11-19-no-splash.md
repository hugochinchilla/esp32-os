# No splash screen, and what the first board turn found in the fade

2026-11-19. Backlog item 74, PR 84, the same branch as
[the start-up fade](2026-11-19-start-up-fade.md).

## What was asked

The previews of the fade began on the splash screen, and the owner had
thought the splash long gone:

> pero no habiamos quitado el splash? Ahh, el splash estaba puesto a cero.
> Ya, pues fuera el splash, no aporta nada, elimina todo, el timer, la
> config, etc. No tendremos splash, un fundido de escritorio desde la
> pantalla de bios es suficiente. Aunque es posible que no llegue a verse
> por el tiempo del monitor en fijar la señal VGA en el reboot de bios a
> os, pero no importa

Minutes later the coordinator relayed the board's report on the fade as
first pushed (batch 22, commit 220597f): two problems, below.

## What was found

- The splash had been hidden since 2026-11-16 (`splash_ms` 0 by default,
  `docs/decisions/start-up-timing.md`), with its code, its two fonts and
  its exact picture test kept. Nothing showed it except `dbg.py boot
  splash` and a board whose `boot.ini` set the time.
- There are two things called splash in the code. The OS's
  (`shell::show_splash`, the mascot and the ribbon of 256 colours) is the
  one the owner means. The BIOS's first screen (`bios::run_splash`) is the
  screen that waits for the monitor, and is "la pantalla de bios" he keeps.
- **The fade never ran on the board.** A start that comes from the BIOS
  sets `boot.fastboot` in the OS, so that the self-test is not repeated,
  and the first version asked for the fade only when the start was not
  fast. Every normal start of a board with a BIOS is such a start. The PC
  has no BIOS stage, so the PC tests could not see it. The board thread
  forced the fade with a one-line patch of its own to measure it.
- With that patch, on the board at 640x480: the colour 259 ms after the
  start, the desktop painted into the kept picture at 524 ms (14.9 ms),
  seven steps of about 36 ms each, one every 60 ms, to 898 ms, the whole
  repaint at 972 ms (50 ms). No refill missed. External memory dips by
  about 312 KB (485 KB at 800x600) and comes back; internal memory does
  not move. 800x600 works. The firmware grew by 4.9 KB. The webcam could
  not see the fade: the monitor is still finding the signal again.
- **A hang, twice out of twice**: with the Bricks pattern on the desktop
  and the board thread's tool asking for screenshots through the fade,
  the watchdog ended the start ("hang, no sign of life for 16 s, Task: ui,
  waiting"). Not with the pattern and no screenshots, and not with
  screenshots and no pattern (three runs).

## What was done

- The OS's splash is removed: `show_splash`, `kSplashMs`, `splash_ms` in
  `BootIo` and `BootSettings` and the line that read it from `boot.ini`,
  the Spleen 32x64 and 12x24 letters, screen 1 of the debug link's Boot
  screen request and `dbg.py boot splash`, the test that compared it with
  the approved drawing, the timing checks in `test_boot`, the scene and
  `00b-boot-splash.png`. `docs/settings.md`, `docs/debug-protocol.md`, the
  two READMEs and the board-turn skill no longer name it. The mascot's
  drawing, which the self-test and the error screen share with it, stays
  as `mascot()`. The approved drawings in `docs/look/boot/` and the
  mock-up scripts stay as the record. A `boot.ini` that still has
  `splash_ms` is read as before and the key is ignored, so nothing on the
  board needs rewriting. `docs/decisions/no-splash.md`.
- The fade is asked for unless a fast start was asked for: `fastboot` in
  `boot.ini`, or the one-shot word of `dbg.py`. Coming from the BIOS no
  longer counts. `firmware/os/main/main.cpp`, `fade_in`.
- The start-up log line loses its "splash N ms" figure.

## The hang: not found

Looked for and not found, so nothing was changed for it:

- On the PC the simulator was started with the Bricks pattern and the
  fade (a local change, not committed) while a script asked for
  screenshots without a pause: 193 screenshots through the self-test, the
  colour, the rise and the desktop, no hang, the right picture at the end.
- Read through for a wait that might not end: the steps of the fade take
  no lock except the remote screen's list of what was painted, as Shut
  Down's steps do; the kept picture comes from one call to the heap; the
  pattern changes only which bytes a step compares. The UI task's loop
  reads at most 4 KB of requests in a round and gives its sign of life
  each round, and a round with one screenshot in it ends.
- What the pattern does change on the board is the size of a screenshot:
  the encoding copies from 1, 2 or 4 pixels back and 1, 2 or 4 lines up,
  and bricks repeat every 8, so some lines of every brick are sent
  unpacked and the screenshot is larger and slower (how much was not
  measured). That points at the screenshots, not at the fade, but it is a
  guess: the same loop of screenshots over a brick desktop that is not
  fading was not among the runs.

The new board request asks for that control run first (the pattern, the
same screenshots, a fast start with no fade, and again on a desktop at
rest), and for the tool and the whole crash record.

## Tested

`tools/gate.sh` passes, 54 of 54. The firmware compiles and links in the
cloud (ESP-IDF v5.5, 1,765,104 bytes). This version has not run on the
board.

## What was learned

- "Fast start" had come to mean two things in the firmware: what the
  debug tool asks for, and "the BIOS has done the screens already". A new
  feature that asked "is this a fast start?" got the second without
  knowing. The PC has no BIOS stage to show it.

## What is left

- The board turn on this version, the control run for the hang first.
- The owner's word on the readings in `docs/decisions/start-up-fade.md`.

## On the board (2026-11-19, 4dd30c0)

App-flashed on the 3 MB layout (`esp32-os.bin` 1,756,928 bytes). A full start
through the BIOS now fades by itself: the plain colour at 159 ms, the desktop
painted into the kept picture at 391 ms (14.9 ms), seven steps of about 36 ms
one every 60 ms, the whole repaint at 839 ms (51 ms); no missed refill; external
RAM dips by 313 KB and comes back. `reboot --fast` shows the desktop at once
(boot to desktop 309 ms). A click on Start as soon as the board answers does
nothing, and 3 s later it opens the menu. A viewer on the remote screen,
connected at 16.9 s, sees the whole desktop. A Start-menu Restart fades too.
`dbg.py boot splash` is refused; `boot selftest` and `boot crash` draw their
screens and `repaint` brings the desktop back.

The hang with the Bricks desktop and screenshots asked from the restart on: the
control runs hang too. Fast restart without the fade: 1 hang of 2; at rest: 0 of
1; with the fade: 3 of 3 (with batch 22, 6 of 8 with Bricks and screenshots, 0
of 6 with the plain desktop, 0 of 3 with Bricks and no screenshots). So the fade
is not the cause; the hang is a finding of its own (a screenshot of the brick
desktop takes about 0.8 s against 0.2 s for the plain one). The records in
`C:\crash.txt` say "hang, no sign of life for 15 or 16 s, ui, waiting (on no
core), uptime 16 s". The owner confirmed the design, and the hang goes to a
thread of its own.
