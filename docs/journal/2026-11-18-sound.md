# Sound: a port, an emulated beeper, a chime and a closing melody

2026-11-18. Backlog items 58 and 59. Built and tested on the PC and in a
browser, by measurement: nobody has heard it yet. On the board it plays
nothing, by design, and nothing here has run on the board.

## 1. What was asked

The owner asked in the project chat whether sound had reached the browser
and SDL builds. It had not: items 58 and 59 were entries only. He gave the
go-ahead for both and added:

> I would like to emulate the beeper too, for the bios boot

The two items, in his words of the same morning: "implement sounds, using a
null sound driver on the board for now, but real implementations on linux
and browser" and "desktop welcome chime, shutdown short melody".

## 2. What was found

- No sound code on `main`. The self-test printed "Detecting sound .......
  none", and the Terminal counted its bells and threw the count away
  ("no sound yet").
- Five recordings chosen by the owner on 2026-11-16 in `docs/sounds/`:
  `boot.pcm` (3.5 s), `close.pcm` (2.9 s), `alert.pcm`, `error.pcm`,
  `bell.pcm`. Raw samples, 16 bits, one channel, 22,050 a second, 390 KB
  together.
- A parked branch, `claude/project-thread-l7amx9` (item 48, PR 51), for a
  real beeper on GPIO14: beep codes for the self-test, a `beep` call on the
  System port, a driver. The owner stopped it ("remove all beeper sound
  from the board. I will review this again when I know what kind of beeper
  I want") after the part that was fitted, an active buzzer, got stuck on
  twice. From it: he accepted 1,000 Hz for the "all is well" beep; the
  error codes were proposed twice and never answered.
- The PC and browser builds are one file, `host/desktop/main.cpp`, on SDL2.
  They show the self-test of `shell::run_self_test`; the board shows the
  BIOS's, `bios::run_post`, from a firmware of its own.
- A browser plays no sound on a page nobody has clicked or typed on.

## 3. The options considered

**Where the beeper lives.**

- A. A second voice of one Sound port, made into samples by a mixer in
  `core/`. The PC tests can check its pitch and length; the board's future
  driver for the audio pin gets it for nothing.
- B. A call of the System port, as the parked branch has it, for a pin
  that is switched on and off. Right for a real beeper, but the PC would
  need a second path into its sound card beside the recordings.
- C. Square waves written in the SDL driver. Least code in `core/`, and
  nothing a test could look at without a sound card.

**Where the recordings live.**

- A. Compiled into the builds that play them, handed to the core at start.
  The board's firmware carries none.
- B. In `core/`. 390 KB of a 1.94 MB slot for sounds the board cannot play.
- C. Files on C:, read when played. The period-true home, and what a
  Sounds settings tool would want. An existing C: of the PC would not have
  them, the board would give 390 KB of C: to silence, and reading a file
  at the moment a box opens is a pause.

**The browser, which will not play before a click.**

- A. The page starts switched off with a power button. One click more, and
  the beep and the chime are heard where they belong.
- B. Start at once. A visitor who only looks never hears the beep or the
  chime; sound begins with the first click.

**What the beeper sounds like.** A bare square wave at 22,050 samples a
second rings at pitches nobody asked for (its edges fall between samples).
The edges are rounded over two samples, and the result goes through two
small filters that stand for a 5 cm speaker in a metal case: little below
350 Hz, rounded off above 6 kHz.

## 4. What was chosen

All of this is the agent's, recorded as `to-confirm` in
`docs/decisions/sound-port.md`; the owner was away while it was built.

- Beeper: A. Recordings: A. Browser: A, put to the owner as a question. He
  answered the same evening: "do A, but show a placeholder "click to start
  Chinchilla 96"". Those are the words under the button now, and a click
  anywhere on the page starts it.
- The beep codes of the parked branch, unchanged, played for the worst
  thing the self-test found. On a PC there is rarely anything to find, so
  what is heard is the one short beep.
- `boot.pcm` when the desktop is up, `close.pcm` with the power-down
  screen, as they were chosen for. Also wired, because the recordings were
  chosen for them and it cost three lines: the knock with message boxes,
  the error sound when there is no memory to open a program, the kalimba
  for the terminal's bell.
- Restart plays nothing: the PC program replaces itself at once, the page
  reloads, the board resets.

## 5. What was built

- `core/include/core/ports.hpp`: `ports::Sound` (`play`, `beep`, `stop`,
  `playing`), `ports::Beep`.
- `core/sound/`: `mixer` (the two voices as samples), `beeps` (the codes),
  `sound` (the list of system sounds, the recordings handed in, an output
  that plays nothing).
- `os::play_sound`, `os::beep`, `os::sound_playing`; `os::install_sound`.
- `shell::BootIo::sound`, `shell::post_beep`, `shell::let_beep_end`; both
  self-tests note the worst result and beep its code.
- `host/desktop/audio.hpp`: the mixer behind an SDL audio device, for the
  Linux program and the browser. `--mute`. The window's close button waits
  for the closing melody.
- `tools/sounds/embed_sounds.py` turns `docs/sounds/` into a C++ file when
  the PC or browser version is built.
- `web/shell.html`: the power button.
- The board: nothing. Its firmwares hand in no output and no recordings, so
  every call ends in `sound::silence()`.

How it was tested:

- `host/tests/test_sound.cpp`: the mixer gives 200 ms of 1,000 Hz within
  3 Hz, with the third harmonic a third as strong and nothing at 1,500 Hz
  or where 20 kHz would fold to; silence 50 ms after; no click at either
  end; a code is bursts and gaps of the right lengths; recordings pass
  through sample for sample; both voices together are limited, not wrapped.
  Both self-tests beep the right code, wait for it and do not wait for
  ever. The desktop plays the chime, the knock, the error and the closing
  melody at their moments.
- `tools/test_desktop_program.py` starts the real program with SDL's
  "dummy" sound card, records what it would play and finds one beep of
  0.2 s at 1,000 Hz, then the chime, equal to `boot.pcm` sample for sample.
- By hand, the same way: a whole start gives the beep 2.5 s in and the
  chime at 7.6 s (after the countdown); closing the window gives the
  closing melody and the program leaves 3.0 s later, when it has ended.
- The browser version was built with Emscripten and run in headless
  Chromium with the samples it sends to the page's audio output counted:
  nothing before the power button; after it the beep at 2.5 s and the
  chime at 7.5 s. `tools/test_web_page.js` (updated for the button) passes.
- The whole PC suite passes (55 tests), the picture comparisons match.

Not tested: by ear, on any machine. A browser that really holds sound back
(the drop after half a second): headless Chromium let the page play even
with its strict policy switched on, so that path has only been read, not
run. Safari and Firefox. Both firmwares (`firmware/os`, `firmware/bios`) were
compiled and linked in the cloud after the first commit; neither has run.

## 6. What was learned

- The recordings are loud next to the beeper: the chime peaks at 29,000 of
  32,767. The beeper is set to 9,000, by judgement, not by ear.
- The memory test of the PC's pretend machine lends its memory once, so a
  test that runs the self-test twice needs a new one each time: six
  failures in the first run of the new test were that, not the beep.
- Chromium keeps a page's permission to play across a reload from the same
  address, so Restart in the browser keeps its sound without the button.
- This cloud machine may not fetch SDL's source from GitHub, which
  Emscripten wants; the copy from Ubuntu's archive (2.30) was used for the
  check. The published page is built by the GitHub workflow, which can.

## On the board (2026-11-19, 113efd7 with main merged in)

App-flashed on the BIOS layout; the BIOS was built only to see that it links
and was not flashed. `esp32-os.bin` is 1,742,080 bytes against 1,735,552 on
main (021a247): +6.5 KB, and the 390 KB of recordings are not in it. The BIOS's
image is 291,168 bytes against 290,640: +528 bytes. Free RAM on the idle
desktop, 191,271 internal and 6,196,044 external, as on main (191,311 and
6,196,364). A normal reboot filmed with the webcam: the Rodent BIOS screen is
as before, with "Detecting sound ....... none", and the OS starts to the
desktop. Start > Run..., a name that does not exist: the "Cannot find" box
opened and closed with OK, nothing else happened. Shut Down reached "It is safe
to power down the system now."; Ctrl+Alt+Del started the system again.
`dbg.py dmesg` has only the fast-start line "Sound: none: OK" (main has it
too) and no other line about sound; `dbg.py misses` is 0. Nothing was written
to C:.
