# The beeper: the BIOS's beep and a chime for the desktop

2026-11-18. Not finished: the chime is not chosen, the error codes are not
confirmed, and nothing here has run on the board. A later entry closes it.

## What was asked

> board is connected again, it has a beeper installed [...] Let's make the
> bios beep on POST as a normal PC, and make a short chime for desktop load
> via the beeper (with design phase, show me options).

This reverses a decision of 2026-11-16, when the beeper was kept for the
modem only and the system sounds went to the audio jack.

## What was found

- The beeper is a piezo disc between 3V3 and GPIO14, no resistor
  (`docs/hardware/README.md`). No firmware drove it.
- A pin gives it one square wave at a time: a pitch, on or off. No chords,
  no volume.
- The Rodent BIOS ends its self-test with "Starting Chinchilla..." and a
  countdown (`core/src/bios/post.cpp`); a fast start skips the BIOS screens.

## The options, and what the owner said

Previews are made by `tools/sounds/make_beeps.py`: the same lists of steps
the firmware plays, through a rough model of a piezo disc (little below
1 kHz, a peak near 3.5 kHz). They give the character, not the real part's
tone. The files are in the project's `look/sounds/beeper/`.

**The beep for "all well".** 1,000 Hz for 200 ms, the PC's pitch, or
2,000 Hz for 120 ms, where a piezo is louder. The owner: "accepted ok-1000".

**Error codes**, proposed in the Award style and not yet confirmed: two
short for a check that failed without stopping the start, three long for
memory, one long and two short for video, one long and three short for
drive C:, long beeps without end when there is no system to start.

**The chime, round one**: five short ones under 0.6 s (a rising arpeggio,
a ta-da, a fast "chip chord", two squeaks, the jack's boot chord note by
note). The owner turned them all down:

> I want something more mellow, calm, not so fast. windows 98 had a quite
> long melody (tan - tararan - chin - chin - chin)

**Round two**: five calmer ones of about 3 s, every note fading like a
struck bar. A pin cannot play quietly, but a narrower pulse sounds
quieter (its fundamental goes as the sine of its width), so the fade is the
pulse narrowed every 20 ms. One of them also plain, to compare.

**Round three**: he then sent a 1995 start-up sound as a reference. It was
measured for its shape only (6.1 s; a soft opening, a swell at 0.85 s, then
notes repeating ever more quietly and brighter) and is not in the
repository or the project folder: the melody has to be ours. Three chimes
of about 4.5 s in that shape: a held note, a small rising figure, then one
bell struck three times, each time quieter.

He then asked for the reference's own melody, adapted to the beeper, and
said he had permission for it. That was not done: the notes are the
protected part of that sound whatever plays them, a permission cannot be
checked from here, and `docs/DECISIONS.md` keeps Microsoft's chime out of
the repository. What was offered instead: the system ships a melody of
ours, and the desktop plays `C:\.config\chime.txt` in its place when the
file holds a tune (`docs/settings.md`), so what plays on his own board is
his to decide and never in the repository.

No answer yet on which chime ships.

## What was built so far

- `ports::Beep` (a pitch, a length, a fade, a starting loudness) and
  `ports::System::beep(steps, count)`: the machine plays the list and keeps
  the time itself, so a busy caller does not make it stumble. Why not a
  port that only sets a pitch, with the core stepping: the chime plays
  while the desktop paints for the first time, and the UI loop's timers are
  late just then.
- `core/sound/beeps.hpp`: the beep codes and the chime (for now candidate A
  of round one, a placeholder).
- The BIOS's self-test notes the worst thing it found and beeps its code
  when the checks are done, over the countdown; a key that skips the
  countdown does not cut the beep. With no system it repeats the long beep.
- `os::beep`, and the desktop plays the chime at the end of `shell::start`:
  the tune in `C:\.config\chime.txt` (`sound::parse_tune`) or the system's.
- `firmware/common/beeper.h`, for both firmwares: the chip's LED PWM makes
  the wave (10 bits, GPIO14, high when silent), a one-shot `esp_timer`
  goes from step to step.
- "Detecting sound" now says "piezo beeper".

Tested on the PC: `test_bios` (the code for each result, silence when not
held), `test_shell` (the chime once when the desktop starts); 44 of 44
pass. The firmware has not been compiled yet, and has not run.
