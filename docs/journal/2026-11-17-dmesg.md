# dmesg: the system log, kept in memory

2026-11-17. The last piece of backlog item 16.

## What was asked

Item 16 lists `dmesg` among the commands. It was left out twice with the
note "waits for a log": the machine's log lines went to the debug link and
nowhere else, so there was nothing to show.

## What was found

- Every log line on the board already passes one point: the firmware hands
  ESP-IDF's log text to `dbg::Protocol::send_log`, which frames it for the
  tool, and drops it if the link is busy.
- init, new today, logs what it starts and which lines of the start-up file
  it could not do. On a machine with no tool attached nobody saw that.

## Options

**Where the log is kept.**

- *Chosen: a ring of bytes in memory, 16 KB, lent by the machine* (external
  RAM on the board). The newest text pushes the oldest out. Lost at a
  restart, like Unix's.
- Not chosen: *a file on C:.* It would survive a restart, and wear the
  flash with every Wi-Fi message; the crash record already keeps what
  matters across a restart.
- Not chosen: *a list of lines.* A ring of bytes needs no limits on a
  line's length or their number.

**When two tasks log at once.**

- *Chosen: the second one's text is dropped*, and counted. Whoever logs is
  never held up by logging; the debug link does the same.
- Not chosen: *wait.* A log line from a task that holds another lock could
  stop the machine.

**What is in it.**

- Everything that goes to the debug link as log text, taken at that one
  point, whether or not the link is listening. What the chip prints before
  the OS starts is not in it.
- `dmesg` shows it oldest first, starting at a whole line, and leaves out
  the colour codes a logger may add. It works into a pipe: `dmesg | grep
  init`, `dmesg | tail`.
- Not built: `dmesg -c` (empty it), following it as it grows, levels.

## What was built

- `core/sys/log.hpp`: `sys::Log` and the machine's one log, `sys::log()`.
- `dbg::Protocol::send_log` keeps each text before sending it.
- `dmesg` in the command shell.
- The board and the simulator lend it 16 KB and a lock. The PC console
  program keeps no log and `dmesg` says so.

## How it was tested

- `host/tests/test_sh.cpp`, `test_log`: the ring (no room, wrap, a reader
  overtaken, a text longer than the room, busy) and `dmesg` (no log, empty,
  colour codes, a pipe).
- The simulator with a start-up file that has a wrong line: `dmesg` on the
  serial console shows init's four lines.
- All 35 PC tests pass; the firmware compiles and links. Not run on the
  board.

## What is left

- On the board: `dmesg` after start (the Wi-Fi and init lines), `dmesg |
  tail`, and that external RAM is 16 KB lower and internal RAM as before.


## On the board (added by the board thread, 2026-11-17)

journal index conflicted; the resolution keeps the board results already on
main and the branch's new lines. PC tests passed (36 of 36; `vnc_tool` failed
once when the tests ran 8 at a time, then passed alone and in a full run one
at a time). The firmware build was clean.

   the three init lines, the Wi-Fi lines, no colour codes left. `dmesg | tail
   -3` and `dmesg | wc` work. (Lines naming Hugo's network are not copied
   here.)
2. Free external RAM on an idle desktop: 6,474,792 bytes against 6,496,072
   on main b74568e, 21,280 bytes lower. The 16 KB ring accounts for 16,384;
   the other 4,896 bytes I could not account for. Internal RAM 210,111
   against 210,403 (0.3 KB less).
3. Logging from several tasks at once: four VNC viewers connecting one after
   another, a Wi-Fi scan, and `dmesg` printed to the Terminal five times
   meanwhile. Nothing hung, nothing was garbled, `dbg.py` kept answering, and
   no refill was late or missed.
